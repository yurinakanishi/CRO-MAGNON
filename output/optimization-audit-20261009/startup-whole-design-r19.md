# Cold start, whole design r19: confirmation to native controls

2026-10-10, runtime026 (Claude, Opus 5.5 Extra, Read/Edit/Write/Glob/Grep only). Read-only design over the current working tree (HEAD 45ec159, clean when this session started) and the adopted r05-a02 measurements. No `src`, `shared`, `scripts`, `tests`, `assets` or `public` file was edited. No command was run, so every **unknown** below stays unknown until Codex runs the two r19 analyzers (§10). startup803's packed-LOD proof and publisher587's lossless PNG proof had no results available to me, and nothing here assumes how they turn out. No KTX, no rejected LOD and no change to approved faces, rigs, clips or collision is proposed.

Notation:

- **Known** means a number in an existing record.
- **Derivable** means arithmetic on known numbers.
- **Unknown** names the measurement that resolves it.
- Per-file sizes are browser `transferBytes` from the r05 r01 page timing (body plus about 300 B of headers).
- Phase totals are the server's completed body bytes. The two measures are not mixed in one sum.
- At 10 Mbps the r05 calibration achieved 2×2 MiB in 3.521 s. Throughput was observed at 1.19–1.21 MB/s during saturated windows, and that range is used for time estimates.

## 0. Result in brief

1. **r01 had two problems, each about 10× over budget.** It reached controls 53.57 s after confirmation:
   - **Bytes:** about 39.8 s of that was saturated transfer. The catalog took 0.75 s, the cave set 12.73 s and the world critical set 26.76 s.
   - **Time with no critical byte in flight:** about 13.8 s. This includes 5.76 s from the last critical byte to world-ready (build, fit, prepare) and 6.04 s from world-ready to controls (Proceed, connect, arrival).

   Shrinking bytes alone cannot reach 5 s. The second component needs attribution, which is Slice 2 (§6).
2. **The title waits for the whole game.** `public/index.html:30` loads `/src/main.js`, whose static closure contains:
   - three: 2,093,209 B;
   - the five dataset modules: 8,357,927 B.

   `main.ts` also constructs the WebGL renderer (`main.ts:498`) before the title appears. The title DOM is generated by `main.ts:265–300` and shown at `main.ts:3338`. Even the contributors dialog and the setup screen statically reach the datasets (§2.1).
3. **The world critical set includes every LOD level of every template.** `world-assets.ts:281–284` awaits `[asset, ...asset.lods]` before installing anything. 11,533,824 B of r01's 32,378,180 B critical world set were LOD-level files that the arrival view does not need. The finer LOD0 can draw instead.
4. **4 MB cannot hold at a camp arrival with exact assets unless exact recompression goes far beyond what is typical** (derivable, §3.4):
   - The camp cave is a landmark 37.2 m from the default arrival focus.
   - It is drawn only after all ten cave images are installed (`world-landmarks.ts:403–404`).
   - So the cave GLB and its ten PNGs (9,706,377 B) belong to the arrival view whether or not the loading cave is shown.
   - The ten PNGs alone are 7,585,869 B. A pixel-exact codec would have to remove more than 47% of them just to equal 4,000,000 B with nothing else sent.

   This design still takes every exact step. It quantifies what remains, and §11 lists the decisions that are the user's, not an engineering choice.
5. **First slice (§5): title shell before the game module.** The title becomes interactive with about 0.46 MB instead of 13.54 MB. Confirmation→controls is unchanged by this slice; the slices that move it follow in §6.

## 2. Mandatory dependencies and why each is awaited

### 2.1 Title: the static import closure

- `public/index.html:30` loads the only entry, `/src/main.js`. Its top level, in order:
  1. awaits `loadMultiplayerConfig()` (`main.ts:182`);
  2. renders the whole application, including the title (`main.ts:280–294`) and setup (`main.ts:295–297`);
  3. constructs `WorldRenderer` (`main.ts:498`, with a WebGL context at `world3d.ts:399–404`, even when `deferWorld`);
  4. calls `showTitle()` last (`main.ts:3338`).
- Every one of `main.ts`'s static imports is fetched and evaluated before that point.
- The datasets are reached through rule modules, not only through `world3d.ts`:

- `main.ts` itself imports:
  - `WORLD/CAMP/NPC` from `world.mts` (`main.ts:44`);
  - `movementFromCamera` from `terrain.mts` (`main.ts:10`);

The authoritative server uses the same modules synchronously; the Durable Object entry is `cloudflare/worker.mts:3`, which imports `world.mjs`.
- **Coast-data consumers.** `isLand/coastDistance` back walking, boats, sea clipping and the world map (`world-map.ts:16`, `paleo-materials.ts:4`). Opening the map is an immediate action, so the coast grid must be whole when controls start. Sharding it would create an incomplete map.

### 2.2 Catalogs

- **Uses.** `world-assets.json` (833,242 B) gives every verified URL, SHA, length and LOD list. It is awaited by:
  - the cave (`loading-cave.ts:118`);
  - `prepareSelf` (`world3d.ts:575`);
  - `initializeWorld` (`world3d.ts:645`).
- **Mascot manifests.** `ACTIVE_MASCOT_MODELS` manifests may be fetched serially inside `loadCatalog` (`world-assets.ts:79–86`).
- **Serial order.** The character manifest is fetched only after the catalog because `loading-cave.ts:118` awaits the catalog before `provider.create`. That costs one serial round, about 0.1 s.

  - `CharacterAnimation` receives every clip at creation (`character-assets.ts:195`).

### 2.4 Cave

- **Images.** The cave needs the template (2,120,508 B) and ten standalone images, 7,585,869 B in total:
  - limestone 2,285,097;
  - mural atlas 1,276,413;
  - 524 frieze 565,302;
  - rimo frieze 560,907;
  - bot-shapes 502,173;
  - maruimo 500,599;
  - friends-river 487,474;
  - friends-meadow 475,363;
  - mae-kohaku 468,527;
  - bot-round 464,014.
- **All-or-nothing install.** `VerifiedTextureBatch` resolves only when all ten are made (`verified-texture.ts:156–183`). Both the loading cave (`loading-cave.ts:157`) and the world landmark (`world-landmarks.ts:245–252`) install all ten or none.
- **Visibility.**
  - The visitor spawns at local z = −20 (`loading-cave-movement.ts:6`).
  - Walking at 2.3 m/s or running at 4 m/s, they stay within z −34…−1 (`loading-cave-movement.ts:13, 18`).
  - Murals sit on both walls from z −7 to −31.4 (`cave-gallery-layout.ts:65–82`).
  - Yaw is free and pitch spans −0.45…0.7 (`loading-cave.ts:274–275`).
  - There is no fog in the cave scene. Every mural centre is within about 14 m of the spawn point.
  - The gallery bends by at most 17.2 m laterally inside the walkable range: `CAVE_BEND` 72.64/38 with smoothstep at t ≤ 0.468 (`camp-cave-layout.mts:18–22`). Nobody has proven occlusion.
  - Partial mural loading therefore cannot guarantee zero blank murals under worst-case camera rotation. All ten stay mandatory for cave-playable; only pixel-exact compression can reduce them.
- **Shared with the world.** At a camp arrival the camp cave is a critical landmark:
  - `CAMP_CAVE` sits at (52, 94) with clearance 50 (`camp-cave-layout.mts:3–14`), 37.2 m from the default focus (48, 57) (`world3d.ts:363`).
  - It is drawn only after its images are installed (`world-landmarks.ts:403–404`).
  - So the loading cave adds no bytes to a camp arrival. It changes order only: the cave group finishes before any world byte is requested (`main.ts:1930–1932`).
- **Unguarded hole.** `prepareEntry` does not require critical landmarks to be instanced (`world3d.ts:734–741`). A world-first path (skipGallery, autostart, exhibition) could therefore report ready while the cave landmark is not drawn. §4 S5 adds the guard.

### 2.6 Build, fit and prepare (5.76 s with no critical transfer)

In `initializeWorld` (`world3d.ts:675–705`), the steps after the last critical byte are:

1. `buildTerrainAssets`, which runs `OpenWorldTerrain.initialize`, the water tiles and the marsh;
2. the forest, camp and motes builders;
3. `WorldLandmarks`;
4. the mountain fit;
5. resource, enemy and actor residency sync;
6. `prepareEntry`: up to `1 + ceil(landmarks/4)` frames, then `compileScene` and one offscreen draw.

The split between these steps is unknown, so it needs timeline marks (Slice 2).

### 2.7 Arrival (6.04 s from world-ready to controls)

The sequence is:

1. Proceed → `enterWorld`, `connect`, `arriveFromGallery` (`main.ts:1910–1914`).
2. `/api/status` with an 8 s timeout (`main.ts:722`).
3. WebSocket (`main.ts:754`) → welcome (`main.ts:771`) → state.
4. `arrival()` waits for `assetsReady && players.get(selfId)` by polling every 50 ms.
5. `openWorld.arrive`.
6. `selfRenderReady`: the actor is attached (`world3d.ts:589–619`).
7. `playReady` (`main.ts:450–451`).

The wait is unattributed. It includes the harness's click latency and main-thread contention from the GLBs streamed at the same time, such as the 4.3 MB mammoth.

### 3.3 Worst-case actor

maruimo-octopus awaits 11,783,380 B before cave-playable. That is 2.18× the woman's 5,412,384 B and about 9.7 s of transfer on its own. Any gate that claims "all selected actors" must be measured with maruimo, not only with the woman.

### 3.4 What 4 MB implies

Arrival content at the camp, for a woman with exact assets: actor 5,412,684 + spears 351,356 + cave 9,706,377 + world LOD0 20,493,000 = **35,963,417 B**. Code, data and catalogs come on top.

- **Floor that cannot shrink.** The actor's 776,340 B of images are already lossy WebP, so no exact re-encode can make them smaller. Images inside the 20.49 MB of world LOD0 files are probably lossy too; their share is unknown (GLB analyzer `byClass.image`).
- **Cave PNGs.** On their own they exceed 4,000,000 B by 3,585,869 B. A pixel-exact codec would have to cut them by more than 47.3% before anything else is counted. publisher587's proof will give the real figure.
- **Conclusion.** With every visible camp object exact, 4 MB needs roughly a 9× shrink of the arrival content. Nothing in the exact toolbox is known to approach that:
  - identical-image and identical-view removal;
  - transport compression of the non-image GLB streams;
  - lossless PNG;
  - narrower exact index widths;
  - collapsing constant clip channels.

  The analyzers turn this into an exact floor. If the floor is above 4 MB, the gap is reported as unmet with its bytes, as `GAME_IMPROVEMENT_PLAN.md:112` requires. Closing it is a content or arrival decision (§11), not something this design takes.

## 4. Split points and invariants

### S1 — Title boundary
**Where:** `public/index.html:30`, `main.ts:265–300`, `main.ts:2687–2723`, `main.ts:3335–3338`, `title-credits.ts:7`.

**Split:**

- A new entry renders the title from a data-free closure.
- The game module (`main.js`) is imported dynamically.
- `main.ts` adopts the existing title node instead of re-rendering it.

**Invariants:**

1. `/src/boot.js`'s static closure contains no `/vendor/three*`, none of the five data modules, no `world3d.js`, no `loading-cave.js` and no `verified-*` module.
2. The title art is requested exactly once.
3. The game import starts at most once.
4. A rejected game import shows an explicit message and a reload button.
5. No WebGL context and no 3D or data request exist before `main.js` evaluates.
6. Importing `/src/main.js` directly, as many `scripts/qa-*.mjs` do, behaves exactly as today.
7. The exhibition, LAN and `?autostart=1` flows are unchanged.

### S2 — Dataset install boundary
**Where:**

- `paleo-geography.mts:1–101`;
- `camp-mountain.mts:2`, `castle-surface.mts:2`, `camp-cave-surface.mts:2`;
- `cave-light.mts:3`, `collision.mts:3,4,9,22`;
- `mountain-materials.ts:12`, `paleo-materials.ts:12`.

**Split:** shared consumers read installed data. There are two install paths:

- The server (Node and the Workers Durable Object) installs synchronously from the existing generated modules at startup.
- The client installs from verified data files (length and SHA, like assets) before any consumer runs.

**Invariants:**

1. A consumer called before install throws; this is test-covered.
2. The client's decoded values equal the module values element by element under `Object.is` (one parity test per dataset).
3. Each dataset installs once per page.
4. The verified cache stays under 64 MiB including datasets.
5. Collision precision never changes. Decimal-scaled integers decode with one correctly rounded division, so they are exact; float32 is not, unless the analyzer proves `float32WouldBeExact`.
6. Order: landmark bounds first (only `collision.mts:9` consumes them), then cave and mountain heights, then the coast grid.

**Scope limits:**

- The coast grid stays whole (§2.1).
- The castle surface may become a region shard only after a test proves every sampler checks bounds before sampling. Its consumers are `terrain.mts`, `collision.mts`, `enemies.mts` and `crow-faction.mts`.

### S3 — Cave image start
**Where:** `loading-cave.ts:122–138`.

## 7. Streaming coverage and failure behavior

- **Turning.**
  - The arrival set is a full disc of radius `STARTUP.visibleRadius` = 118 + 16√2 ≈ 140.6 m, measured from the camera and widened by the maximum camera-to-player distance. Fog is opaque at 118 m (`startup-plan.ts:15–19`).
  - Every object with a point inside the disc is installed before controls, at the finest level available (S4). Any camera rotation therefore shows complete objects.
  - Partial cave murals are rejected (§2.4).
- **Moving.**
  - After controls, an object outside the arrival set at footprint-edge distance d from the arrival point must be installed by t(d) = (d − R − c) / v_max. Here R = 140.6 m, c is the camera offset, and v_max = 7 m/s (fast boat; on foot 6.4 m/s).
  - The streaming order is feasible if, for every d, the cumulative bytes of objects nearer than d (beyond the arrival set) are at most 1.19 MB/s × t(d).
  - A planner test computes this from catalog bytes and placements for the camp and for every `spawnSite`.
  - LOD levels have no deadline, because the finer level substitutes.
  - The mountain's level 1 has an 11.5 s lead and level 0 substitutes for it.
- **Failure.**
  - A failed integrity check or decode stops the world explicitly, as `failWorld` does today.
  - A slow network makes the client withhold movement input that would bring the camera within R of an object that is not yet installed. It shows a visible "地形を読み込んでいます" status; the server position is never hidden or faked, and loading is not disguised as slow walking.
  - In the 10 Mbps worst-case scripted run this wait must trigger zero times, or the slice fails. That run turns 360° every 2 s while moving at v_max toward the largest pending bytes, and asserts `missingVisible = 0` every frame.
  - A warp or spawn (a jump of at least 32 m, `startup-plan.ts:21`) is a new arrival with its own set.

## 8. Does shared bufferView compression repeat data?

- **Per-view compression.** EXT_meshopt_compression stores one compressed range per bufferView: `buffer`, `byteOffset`, `byteLength`, `byteStride`, `count`, `mode` and `filter` on the view's extension. three r185 decodes per view:
  - It reads the range and decodes `count * byteStride` bytes (`node_modules/three/examples/jsm/loaders/GLTFLoader.js:1605–1671`).
  - It caches every dependency as `type:index` (`GLTFLoader.js:2872–2875`).
  - Accessors get their view through that cache (`GLTFLoader.js:3093`).

  So accessors that share one view are stored and decoded once, and their sharing repeats nothing.
- **Where repetition can happen:**
  1. Different views whose decoded bytes are identical. Both are stored and transferred.
  2. Identical images or data in different files. Every LOD file and every template is downloaded and parsed separately (`world-assets.ts:281–286`). Runtime `coalesceTextures` merges GPU textures only inside one template cohort after decoding (`embedded-glb.ts:394–399`), so it saves GPU memory, not transfer.
  3. Two views pointing at the same compressed range would be transferred once but decoded twice.

  The GLB analyzer reports each case: `identicalDecodedViews`, `identicalImages`, and `bin.identicalStoredRanges` / `overlappingBytes`.
- **Transport compression.** The meshopt README says the codec output "can also be further compressed in transport using a general-purpose compression algorithm such as Deflate" (`node_modules/meshoptimizer/README.md:61`). That is why non-image GLB streams get measured gzip and brotli sizes; images are not recompressed.

## 11. Decisions that are not engineering choices (for the user)

**D1 — Arrival composition versus 4 MB.** With every visible object exact, a camp arrival needs roughly a 9× shrink (§3.4). The available paths are:

- accept "unmet" with the measured floor;
- choose a different first-arrival spawn whose view set is small;
- define a declared rule for objects inside the fog radius that may appear after controls;
- accept a separate startup texture tier.

The last two conflict with "単一画質" and the rejected 512 results. Nothing here assumes one of them.

**D2 — Other visible actors at controls.** Today they are not awaited: the NPC (8,091,164 B) and the herd (4,337,932 B). The plan's "まだ見えない人物" wording suggests visible people should be present. Including them makes D1 harder.

**D3 — Game module start.** The shell can start downloading the game module immediately after the title is interactive (recommended; it changes no byte total and does not delay the title), or only on the first title action, following "開く操作に合わせて読む" literally.

**D4 — Joining earlier.** Joining the room before Proceed, as S8 could, would show the visitor to other players while they are in the cave.

**D5 — Transport compression in the product's own servers.** This is Slice 8. Any deployment still needs separate explicit authorization.
